Assembly

1
Do not rush this

Cut 8 fibres, each about 70 cm (box → top of head → down the braid, plus slack). Cut with a fresh razor blade against a hard surface — never scissors. Scissors crush the core and the end scatters light instead of accepting it. Then sand the LED end flat on fine paper and flame-polish it briefly until it goes glassy. This single surface is the biggest brightness lever in the whole build.

2
Make the one hard part once

Drill 8 holes in one block — Delrin, aluminium, or a print — at exactly your strip's pixel pitch (144/m ≈ 6.9 mm; measure yours). Size them so fibre press-fits snugly. The fibre end must physically touch the pixel's face: an air gap throws light away. Wrap the whole junction in reflective tape or white heatshrink to bounce stray photons back into the fibre. Doing all eight couplings as one part is the entire reason this build is tractable.

3

Cut an 8-pixel run from your APA102 strip and solder four wires to its input end: 5V, GND, DI, CI. The strip has arrows printed on it — data flows one way only. Solder to the end the arrows point away from, or nothing lights and you will blame the code.

4
The hour-eating mistake lives here

Power the shifter at 5V (single-supply — that's how it reads 3.3 V as HIGH and puts out clean 5 V). Add a 0.1 µF across its Vcc/GND. Tie pins 1 and 4 (/OE) to GND. They are active-low; leave them floating and the outputs stay dark while every wire looks perfect. This is the single most common way to lose an evening on this build.

5

Wire the mic for glitch mode. L/R to GND puts it on the left channel — your I²S read expects that. Mount it so the little port hole faces out of the box toward the music, not into a wiring bundle.

6
Brownout insurance

Feed pixel 5 V from the rail directly — never through the ESP32's regulator. Star all grounds to one point. Then the caps: 1000 µF across the rail near the pixels, 470 µF across the ESP32's 3V3/GND. That second one is not optional — ESP32 WiFi transmit spikes hard, and without it you get Brownout detector was triggered resets mid-show, on playa, in the dark.

7
Before glue. Before coat. Before anything.

Flash it, join the AP from your phone, cycle every mode, and check that all eight strands light and match. A dull strand means a bad coupling — pull that fibre, re-polish, re-seat. Do this while everything is still open and reversible. Every mistake in this build is cheap right now and expensive in four steps' time.

8

Brush conformal coat over the board and solder joints — mask the mic port, the connectors, and the pixel faces. Drop silica gel in the box. Grommet and hot-glue every cable exit. Playa dust is alkaline and mildly conductive; uncoated joints start failing around day four.

9
Cord takes the load. Fibre takes none.

Bundle the paracord and all 8 fibres, then sleeve the lot in black heatshrink — that's your chain. The paracord is anchored at both ends and carries every gram; the fibre floats loose inside carrying nothing. If the fibre ever takes the box's weight, it will creep, whiten, and dim over a week of movement. Add strain relief where the chain enters the box and where it meets the braid — both flex points.

10

Fit the magnetic breakaway in the chain between box and head. Braid the 8 fibres into tight tracks against the scalp so the surrounding hair conceals them and only the glow shows. The breakaway is a genuine safety part — anything worn around the neck in a crowd of bikes and hugs must come apart when it snags, not pull your head with it.

Do not

A metal enclosureFaraday cage. Your AP dies inside it and you will debug firmware for a night. Plastic only, and keep the antenna end clear.

A LiPo pouch on your neckCrush, puncture, dust-short — and a cell that vents does it against your throat. Power bank on the belt, always.

Scissors on fibreA crushed end scatters light back out instead of accepting it. Razor, sand, flame-polish. Every time.

Floating /OE pinsPins 1 and 4 to GND. Otherwise: perfect wiring, correct code, zero light, four lost hours.

Fibre bearing the box's weightAnchor the paracord at both ends. Loaded fibre creeps and dims over a week of movement.

Sealing before you bench-testStep 7 exists so that a bad coupling costs you a minute, not a rebuild. Nothing gets glued until all eight strands light.
