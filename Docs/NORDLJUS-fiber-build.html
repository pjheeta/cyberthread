How it fits together

The coupler — in detail

A fibre makes no light of its own. It is a pipe: light shone into its end travels the length and (side-glow fibre) leaks out along the way, so the strand glows. The coupler is the junction where an SK9822 pixel's face meets a fibre's end. Get this right and the piece is bright and controllable. Get it wrong and no amount of good code or good fibre recovers it. Everything below is that junction, magnified.

One coupler, in cross-section — repeated eight times across the block

The insight that makes it buildable: the block pitch is the strip pitch

You are not aligning eight fibres to eight LEDs by eye. The SK9822 strip already spaces its pixels at a fixed, repeating distance — its pixel pitch. Drill your block's holes at that exact spacing and every bore lands over a pixel automatically. Measure yours center-to-center: a 30 LED/m strip is about 33 mm pitch, so eight pixels span roughly 233 mm. That makes the block a long, slim bar — design the enclosure around a bar, not a cube. (If you want a compact block instead, cut the strip into single pixels and rejoin them with short DAT/CLK/5V/GND jumpers at a tighter pitch — more soldering, smaller part. Keeping the strip intact is the low-effort path.)

The four things that must be true at every bore

Do this
Polished flat end, bottomed out, touching the lens, dry.
Not this
Air gap. Light spreads and misses the core — dim strand.
Not this
Crushed/scissor-cut end. Scatters light back out.
Not this
Glue in the gap. Fogs the lens, permanently dims it.

Coupler assembly, bore by bore

1 · Prep the fibre end. Cut each fibre with a fresh razor against a hard surface — never scissors, which crush the core. Sand the end flat on fine paper, then pass it briefly through a flame until it goes glassy. This polished face is the single biggest brightness lever in the build.

2 · Bottom it out, dry. Press the polished end into its bore until it stops on the shelf, resting against the pixel's emitting face. There must be nothing between fibre and lens — no glue, no air you can help. Plastic touching plastic.

3 · Retain from behind. Put a small bead of adhesive on the fibre shaft where it exits the back of the block, never at the tip. The block sets the aim and distance; the glue only stops the fibre backing out.

4 · Wrap the junction. Sleeve the whole block-to-strip zone in reflective tape or white heatshrink. Stray light that would escape sideways gets bounced back into the fibres — free brightness, and it stops light leaking between neighbouring channels and muddying your per-strand colour.

5 · Keep it re-seatable. During bench test you will find a dull strand. A dry friction-fit lets you pull that one fibre, re-polish, and re-seat in thirty seconds. A fibre glued to the lens cannot be fixed. This is why steps 2 and 3 keep the optical face dry.

Full build sequence

1
Test the idea before you build it

Before any box or block: wire one SK9822 pixel to the ESP32, press one polished fibre against it in a dark room, and look. This is the fifteen-minute test that tells you whether glowing fibre reads the way you're picturing. Fibre always looks dim in daylight and great in the dark — judge it where you'll wear it. If it's too dim even in the dark, fix the polish or the contact now, not after you've built eight of them.

2

Cut an 8-pixel run from the SK9822 strip. Solder four wires to its input end — 5V, GND, DAT, CLK. SK9822 is directional: the strip has arrows, and data flows one way. Solder to the end the arrows point away from, or nothing lights.

3

Wire the strip to the ESP32 over hardware SPI — the same DotStar setup you used on the bike wheel. In FastLED: FastLED.addLeds<SK9822, DATA_PIN, CLOCK_PIN, BGR>(leds, 8);. Because SK9822 carries its own clock, it shrugs off the jostling a wearable takes — no timing glitches when you move.

4
The one part you fabricate

Drill the coupling block at your strip's pixel pitch, one bore per pixel, each sized for a snug fibre press-fit with a shelf at the bottom so the fibre bottoms out against the lens without crushing it. Black/opaque material — a 3D print, Delrin, or black acrylic. See The coupler above; this is the step that decides everything.

5

Couple all eight fibres — prep, bottom out dry, retain from behind, wrap. Cut fibres long and polish after seating so any insertion damage sands away. Mount the block over the strip so each bore sits centred on its pixel.

6
Optional — glitch mode

Add the INMP441 mic if you want sound reactivity: VDD→3V3 (not 5V), L/R→GND, and the I²S trio to free GPIOs. On WLED, the AudioReactive usermod drives it for you. Mount the port facing out of the box toward the music.

7
Before you close anything

Power it on the bench, run every mode, and confirm all eight strands light and match. A dull one is a coupling — pull, re-polish, re-seat. Do this while everything is open and a fix costs a minute. Then run it on battery at full brightness for an hour and watch for heat and for how long the power bank actually lasts.

8

Build the chain: bundle the eight fibres with a 550 paracord core and sleeve the lot in black heatshrink. The paracord is anchored at both ends and carries all the weight; the fibres float loose inside carrying none — loaded fibre creeps and dims over a week. Fit the magnetic breakaway so a snag pops apart instead of pulling your head.

9

Braid the eight fibres into tight tracks against the scalp so surrounding hair hides the strands and only the glow shows. Strain-relieve where the chain enters the box and where it meets the braid — both are flex points that fatigue over the week.
